## Overview

The United Arab Emirates sits at the crossroads of ancient trade routes connecting Mesopotamia, the Indus Valley, and the Arabian Peninsula. Archaeological evidence confirms human presence in the region for **over 300,000 years** — from Lower Palaeolithic hunter-gatherers to Bronze Age seafarers and Iron Age metalworkers. Recent excavation seasons (2023–2025) have dramatically expanded our understanding of these civilisations, aided by modern technologies such as ground-penetrating radar (GPR) and magnetic surveys.

## 🏙️ Dubai Discoveries

Dubai Culture and Arts Authority (Dubai Culture) manages over **17 significant archaeological sites** across the emirate, spanning from the Lower Palaeolithic era to the late Islamic period.

---

### 1. Saruq Al-Hadid (2600–550 BC)

**Location:** Desert interior of Dubai  
**Discovered:** 2002 by HH Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum  
**Period:** Bronze Age to Iron Age  

One of the most important and expansive archaeological sites in the Arabian Peninsula. Believed to have been a **major Iron Age metallurgical centre**, Saruq Al-Hadid has yielded thousands of rare artefacts, including:

- Bronze, ceramic, and stone vessels
- Weapons: daggers, swords, axes, iron and bronze arrowheads
- Adornments: decorated seashells, thousands of beads from precious and semi-precious stones
- Local and foreign seals
- Unique gold and silver artefacts
- Evidence of charcoal manufacturing (from the Saruq-53 area)

#### 🔬 2025 Technology Breakthrough
In **February 2025**, Dubai Culture — in collaboration with **Khalifa University of Science and Technology** — deployed advanced non-invasive technologies at the site:
- **Ground-Penetrating Radar (GPR)**
- **Magnetic survey**
- **High-precision topographic mapping**

Results:
- Detection and localisation of **over 400 anomalies** of potential archaeological value
- Identification of **5 principal buried structures**
- **6 potential archaeological areas** identified within the main site
- A **30% reduction** in excavation time and costs

---

### 2. Al Sufouh (2500–2000 BC)

**Location:** Al Sufouh district, Dubai  
**Discovered:** 1988  
**Period:** Umm Al-Nar civilisation (3rd millennium BC)

Excavations (1994–1995) revealed **three earth-cut tombs** from the Umm Al-Nar period, including:
- A circular tomb (up to 6.5 m in diameter) built of trimmed stone blocks
- Skeletal remains, copper weapons, stone and pottery vessels
- Beads, shells, and cylinder seals

---

### 3. Al Qusais (2500–550 BC)

**Location:** Al Qusais area, Dubai  
**Discovered:** 1970s  
**Period:** Bronze and Iron Ages

A large multi-period settlement featuring:
- A cemetery containing remains of approximately **120 individuals**
- Communal graves
- Metal weapons, pottery, stone vessels, jewellery, beads, shells
- Unique bronze snake models

---

### 4. Jumeirah Islamic City (900–1800 AD)

**Location:** Jumeirah, Dubai  
**Discovered:** 1969  
**Period:** Abbasid Caliphate era (10th century AD onward)

Remains of an ancient Islamic city, including:
- **12 buildings**: 9 residential, 1 caravanserai, 1 mosque, 1 market
- Islamic architecture with geometric and floral gypsum decorations
- Evidence of Jumeirah's role as a major **commercial hub** linking Oman, the Arabian Peninsula, Mesopotamia, and the Far East

---

### 5. Margham Site (1300–600 BC)

**Location:** Margham area, Dubai  
**Discovered:** Recently (reported 2024)  
**Period:** Late Bronze Age / Iron Age

A newly discovered site containing:
- A **semi-circular tomb** resembling Hafit-era architecture (3200–2500 BC)
- A burial chamber (1.6 m long × 96 cm wide) with a skeleton
- Funerary gifts: softstone bowls, flintstone cores, a small bronze cup
- **10 bronze arrowheads**

---

### 6. Jabal Al Yamh Tombs (2500–1300 BC)

**Location:** Hatta, Dubai  
**Period:** Hafit, Umm Al Nar, Wadi Suq, and Iron Age civilisations

- **84 tombs** documented and recorded beneath mountain slopes
- Artefacts: pottery vessels, softstone vessels, decorated shells, copper rings, carnelian, faience, stone and glass beads
- ~29 tombs restored; comprehensive conservation plan underway for the remainder

---

### 7. Wadi Jima & Suhaila Sites

**Location:** Hatta, Dubai  
**Period:** Late Islamic Era (17th–19th century AD)

- **Wadi Jima**: An Islamic agricultural village with residential buildings, agricultural terraces, and a complex network of irrigation channels (support walls, retaining walls, dams)
- **Suhaila**: Several villages sharing similar architectural elements; includes the 'Hatta Islamic Village' — a hilltop cluster of houses and a mosque

---

### 8. Al Ashoush Site (3rd Millennium BC)

**Location:** Inland desert, Dubai  
**Period:** 3rd millennium BC

One of the most unique sites in Dubai — representing early human settlements in the **remote inland desert**, far from the Arabian Gulf coast.

## 🕌 Abu Dhabi Discoveries

The Department of Culture and Tourism – Abu Dhabi (DCT Abu Dhabi) operates one of the most ambitious archaeology programmes in the region, with **seven active excavation sites** as of 2024.

---

### 1. Sas Al Nakhl / Umm an-Nar (2700–2000 BC)

**Location:** Sas Al Nakhl Island (Umm an-Nar), Abu Dhabi  
**Season:** 2023–2024  
**Period:** Umm an-Nar Bronze Age culture

This island gave its name to the **Umm an-Nar Bronze Age culture** — the first site of this era excavated in the region. Recent excavations revealed:

- **Bitumen from ancient Mesopotamia** (modern Iraq) used to waterproof pottery and a clay-lined storage pit
- A large bitumen fragment bearing impressions of wood and rope — likely part of a **Bronze Age boat hull**
- A well-preserved assemblage of **over 30,000 animal bones** (fish and seabirds dominating the diet)
- Large animal bones around a circular fireplace — suggesting **ritualistic or communal activity**
- Bones fashioned into spindles and spatulas
- Evidence of significant international trade with **Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley (2800–2200 BCE)**

---

### 2. Mutaredh Oasis (Multi-Period)

**Location:** Western edge of Mutaredh Oasis, Abu Dhabi  
**Discovered:** 2023 (during a construction project)  
**Published:** October 2024 (*Frontiers in Earth Science*)

A **major new site** discovered accidentally during construction. Features documented include:
- An **earthen mosque** and boundary walls
- Iron Age **irrigation systems (falaj)**
- A **circular stone tomb** dating to the Bronze Age

A subsequent **GPR survey** of the unexplored eastern sector (Khalifa University, 2024) identified:
- Two additional **Bronze Age circular tombs**
- A system of **water channels (falaj)** and Iron Age irrigation networks with tree pits
- Walls from the **Late Islamic Age**

The site shows intensive human activity from the **Bronze Age through to the Late Islamic period**.

---

### 3. Ghagha Island (8,500 Years Old)

**Location:** Ghagha Island (westernmost island of the UAE), Abu Dhabi  
**Season:** 2023–2024

DCT Abu Dhabi archaeologists have discovered **8,500-year-old structures** on this remote island — among the oldest built structures discovered in the UAE.

---

### 4. Delma Island (7,000 Years Old)

**Location:** Delma Island, Abu Dhabi  
**Season:** 2023–2024

Ongoing excavations uncovering a **7,000-year-old settlement**, providing evidence of early coastal communities in the Arabian Gulf.

---

### 5. Sir Bani Yas Monastery

**Location:** Sir Bani Yas Island, Al Dhafra Region, Abu Dhabi

DCT Abu Dhabi revealed a newly discovered **cross moulded on a stucco plaque** from the ancient pre-Islamic Christian monastery — a vivid reminder of the UAE's diverse religious heritage before the advent of Islam.

## ⚱️ Umm Al Quwain Discoveries

### 1. Tell Abraq (2500 BC – 300 AD)

**Location:** Tell Abraq (Til Abrook), Umm Al Quwain  
**Season:** 2023–2024 (concluded December 2024)  
**Led by:** Prof. Daniel Potts (NYU) & Italian Mission in Umm Al Quwain

A multi-period site inhabited from the **Umm Al Nar Bronze Age (~2600–2000 BCE)** through to approximately **300 AD**. The 2023–2024 season's headline discoveries:

- A **possible pre-Islamic shrine** with an open-air altar
- A **stone block with an Aramaic inscription**
- A pottery jar containing **"imitation" Roman coins** (locally minted bronze coins imitating the image of Emperor **Tiberius, 14–37 AD**), locally minted bronze coins, and a bronze bracelet — likely a **religious offering**
- Incense burners supporting the shrine theory
- Previous finds: stone statues, bronze and clay figurines

> *"The coins are among the most important archaeological discoveries of the season. They are tangible material evidence of the qualitative economic leaps achieved by the Tell Abraq archaeological site."*  
> — Rania Hussein Kannouma, Acting Director of Archaeology & Heritage, UAQ Department of Tourism and Archaeology

The imitation Roman coins point to a significant phase of **Indian Ocean globalisation** during the 1st–3rd centuries AD.

## 🛰️ Technology in UAE Archaeology

Modern UAE archaeology is increasingly leveraging cutting-edge, non-invasive technology:

| Technology | Application | Site(s) |
|---|---|---|
| **Ground-Penetrating Radar (GPR)** | Detecting buried structures & artefacts without excavation | Saruq Al-Hadid, Mutaredh Oasis |
| **Magnetic Survey** | Identifying metallic/non-metallic anomalies underground | Saruq Al-Hadid |
| **High-Precision Topographic Mapping** | 3D modelling of site layout | Saruq Al-Hadid |
| **Remote Sensing & Satellite Imagery** | Broad-area site identification | Multiple UAE sites |
| **Zooarchaeology** | Diet and lifestyle reconstruction from faunal remains | Sas Al Nakhl |
| **Isotopic / Provenance Analysis** | Tracing trade goods (bitumen, copper, etc.) to origin regions | Sas Al Nakhl, Tell Abraq |
